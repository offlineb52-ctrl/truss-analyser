Display & material

EA is the same for every member. It only affects deflections: in a statically determinate truss the forces come from equilibrium alone and do not depend on stiffness.

How the solver works

Equations. Every joint is a frictionless pin, so each one gives two equilibrium equations, ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0. With j joints that makes 2j equations.

Unknowns. The unknowns are one axial force per member (m) plus one per reaction component (r). A pin gives 2 components, a roller gives 1.

Matrix. A tensile force T in a member from joint a to joint b pulls a towards b. With unit vector (cx, cy) from a to b, it adds +cxT and +cyT to joint a's equations and −cxT and −cyT to joint b's. Gathering the coefficients gives A·x = −P. Each row is one equation, each column is one unknown, and P holds the applied loads.

Stability. A unique solution needs as many independent equations as unknowns, so the first check is 2j = m + r. That condition is necessary but not sufficient, because geometry can make equations repeat each other (for example, all reactions parallel). The rank of A settles it: rank < 2j means a mechanism, and rank < m + r means redundant members.

Solve. Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting, written by hand in solver.js.

Deflections. By virtual work, member extensions and joint displacements are related by the transpose of the same matrix: Aᵀ·u = −e, with e = TL/EA.
